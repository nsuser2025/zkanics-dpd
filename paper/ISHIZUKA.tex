\section{INTRODUCTION}
On a rainy day, raindrops on a car window merge together and race down the glass like shooting stars.
Droplet coalescence on solid surfaces is a fascinating interfacial phenomenon
in which the interplay between fluid–fluid and fluid–solid interfaces
governs the resulting droplet dynamics.
Particularly on superhydrophobic surfaces, this phenomenon exhibits a dramatic behavior:
instead of simply sliding along the surface, the coalesced droplet
can spontaneously jump away from it.
This phenomenon, commonly referred to as coalescence-induced droplet jumping,
has attracted considerable attention both as a fascinating interfacial phenomenon
and for its potential industrial applications.

The jumping dynamics are governed by the interplay among capillary forces,
viscous dissipation, inertia, and droplet--surface interactions.
Previous studies have primarily focused on the effects of macroscopic properties,
such as surface wettability, droplet size, viscosity, and surface tension,
on coalescence-induced droplet jumping.
In continuum descriptions, droplets are generally treated as homogeneous fluids,
and microscopic variations in particle properties within the droplet
are not explicitly considered.
Consequently, it remains unclear how microscopic particle-level properties,
such as particle mass and the presence of impurities,
affect the conversion of interfacial energy into the translational motion
of the coalesced droplet.

Mesoscopic particle-based simulations provide a useful framework
for addressing this question because they can describe hydrodynamic behavior
while retaining explicit particle degrees of freedom.
In particular, many-body dissipative particle dynamics (MDPD)
can reproduce liquid--vapor phase separation and provide a convenient
particle-based description of liquid droplets and their interactions with solid surfaces.
The explicit representation of individual particles also makes it possible
to independently vary particle properties, such as mass,
and to introduce a small number of particles with different properties
as impurities.
This provides a route to investigate how microscopic heterogeneity
affects the macroscopic dynamics of droplet coalescence and jumping.

In this work, we use MDPD simulations to investigate the roles of particle mass
and impurities in coalescence-induced droplet jumping on solid surfaces.
Two droplets are first equilibrated independently on a solid surface
and are then brought into close proximity to initiate coalescence.
By systematically varying the particle mass and introducing impurities,
we examine their effects on the post-coalescence droplet dynamics.
In particular, we analyze the jumping velocity and direction
and relate the observed behavior to momentum and energy transfer during coalescence.
Our results demonstrate how particle-level properties,
which are not explicitly represented in conventional continuum descriptions,
can influence the macroscopic dynamics of coalescing droplets.
These findings provide a particle-level perspective on the physical mechanisms
governing coalescence-induced droplet jumping on solid surfaces.\clearpage
